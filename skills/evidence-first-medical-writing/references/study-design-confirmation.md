# Confirm study design before selecting reporting guidance

## Purpose and boundary

Run this gate before finalizing design-dependent reporting guidance for substantive writing or review. A manuscript title or an author's preferred label is a starting claim, not proof. Authors need not know methodological terminology: ask what actually happened, then explain the supported classification in plain language.

Reuse factual confirmations already recorded in the task card/Manuscript Contract. Do not repeat intake questions for each section, reviewer, or round. Reopen only facts affected by new evidence, a contradiction, or a changed research question, analysis, or report type. Pure copyediting and literal translation retain the exceptions in [reporting-guideline-retrieval.md](reporting-guideline-retrieval.md).

This is a reporting-selection gate, not permission to redesign a completed study, change data, alter a registration/protocol, or conduct an additional analysis. Design confirmation does not establish scientific validity, ethical compliance, or causal identification.

## 1. Offer a short, evidence-based proposal

Read the available Methods, protocol/registration, chronology, and relevant Contract facts before asking questions. Distinguish documented facts, author-confirmed facts, and assistant inferences; note sources and locations. Absence of a method from the manuscript means unreported, not necessarily not performed.

Use a brief exchange such as:

> My provisional classification is [design/report type], because [two concrete facts and their sources]. I cannot yet tell [decisive unknown]. This affects [guideline choice or interpretation]. Please confirm or correct the factual summary; you do not need to choose a technical label.

If the supplied facts already support a classification, ask for confirmation of that short summary, not a full questionnaire. An existing fact-based confirmation in the Contract satisfies this step unless something material has changed. Do not record author confirmation from silence, an unverified manuscript label, or the assistant's own inference.

## 2. Ask only decisive questions

Ask 1–3 focused, plain-language questions per turn, choosing only those that could change classification or reporting scope. Explain the distinction briefly when helpful. These are a question bank, not a mandatory intake form:

| Decision | Focused factual question | What to distinguish |
|---|---|---|
| Intervention assignment | Did the research team assign an intervention under the study plan, or did treatment/exposure arise through routine care, participant choice, or another process? Who decided, and when? | Research-assigned intervention versus observation of care/exposure; receiving treatment alone does not make a trial. |
| Random allocation | What exact process determined who received each intervention: a chance-generated sequence, alternation, admission date/record number, clinician choice, or something else? What was allocated: individuals, groups, or another unit? | Random allocation is not random sampling, balanced group sizes, matching, or alternation. A claim of randomization needs the actual allocation mechanism; missing concealment/blinding details are separate reporting and bias questions. |
| Selection into the analysis | Were participants sampled because of their outcome status, or was a population/exposure-defined group identified and its outcomes assessed? What was the source population and selection rule? | Case-control sampling versus a cohort; a one-time population snapshot may instead support cross-sectional classification. Describe nested or other variants explicitly. |
| Planning and chronology | When was this research question/plan set, when were people selected, and when were exposures and outcomes measured or retrieved? Which outcomes had already occurred or were known at those stages? | Original data generation, collection/retrieval, study planning, and analysis are different dates. Records do not automatically mean a retrospective cohort; routine future follow-up does not automatically mean a prospective study. |
| Current report and comparison | Is this a protocol, completed-results report, or secondary analysis? Which groups/variables does this manuscript actually compare, and was that comparison defined before outcomes were examined? | The current analysis may differ from the source study. Trial provenance alone does not make a biomarker association, post-randomization subgroup comparison, or other secondary contrast randomized. |
| Evidence synthesis | Is this a narrative review, systematic review, scoping review, another synthesis, or its protocol? How were sources searched, selected, and synthesized? | Confirm the actual review methods and purpose; a “review” label or the presence/absence of meta-analysis alone is insufficient. |

For randomized-trial terminology, consult the official [CONSORT explanation and elaboration](https://www.bmj.com/content/389/bmj-2024-081124) and retrieve the applicable current documents at runtime. The [STROBE explanation and elaboration](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.0040297) cautions that prospective/retrospective terminology is ambiguous: report the actual sequence and, if using either label, define its meaning.

Do not force a mixed or embedded design into one label. Separate components, populations, allocation units, time periods, and analysis purposes when they need different guidance. A cohort combining historical records and newly collected follow-up needs that chronology stated; a diagnostic or prediction analysis embedded in a trial needs its own purpose identified. Use applicable guidance for the report and components, not the prestige of the parent design.

## 3. Confirm facts without manufacturing agreement

Show the updated classification and concise factual basis after the author answers. Record whether the author confirmed the summary or corrected particular facts. They confirm what happened; a preferred label cannot override an incompatible allocation mechanism, sampling rule, or timeline. If sources conflict, preserve both accounts, identify the decisive discrepancy, and ask for source verification rather than silently replacing either account.

Retain `Unknown` for facts the author cannot establish. If the author is unavailable, refuses clarification, or a decisive contradiction remains, keep the classification provisional or unresolved. Do not infer missing methods, require patient-level data merely to pass this gate, or use pressure to obtain agreement. Recommend a methods specialist when the design remains genuinely ambiguous.

Unresolved design does not stop all useful work. Continue fact extraction, design-neutral structure/language work, and clearly labeled candidate-guideline retrieval. Do not finalize design-dependent drafting, causal interpretation, checklist applicability, or a compliance pass while the facts needed for that decision remain unresolved. A non-decisive reporting gap can stay open without blocking a supported design classification; state its separate assessment limit.

## 4. Record the Design Confirmation Card

Store this compact card in the task card/Manuscript Contract and link it from the Decision Ledger when one exists. Use stable source locations and the author's actual response, not an invented sign-off.

```text
Design card ID / version / date:
Manuscript or analysis version and report purpose:
Proposed or confirmed classification (components and current contrast):
Concrete supporting facts and source locations:
Facts already confirmed and reused from the Contract:
Unresolved facts / contradictions / why they matter:
Author confirmation or correction: response/date/source, or Not obtained
Status: Provisional / Awaiting author clarification / Confirmed / Unresolved
Guidance consequence: eligible candidates or finalized selection; withheld decisions
Change from prior version and affected guideline/review/draft items:
```

Use `Confirmed` only when the decisive factual basis has author confirmation, supports the stated classification, and has no unresolved contradiction material to it. Confirmation can coexist with explicitly recorded non-decisive unknowns. A proposed future design is confirmed only as a plan, never as completed conduct.

## 5. Hand off to guideline retrieval and revision

After confirmation, follow [reporting-guideline-retrieval.md](reporting-guideline-retrieval.md) to verify the appropriate main guideline, extensions, protocol/results scope, and journal requirements. Link the card version to the Guideline Manifest. Candidate documents retrieved earlier are not automatically approved for use; verify their fit against the confirmed facts.

If later evidence changes the classification, version the card, record the reason and author correction, and revisit affected guidance, manuscript labels, and review judgments. Do not silently switch criteria mid-review: update the clean guidance package consistently for the next applicable round. Preserve the prior decision trail and all real study facts; propose truthful reporting corrections without editing source data, registrations, or historical protocols.
