# AI-use policy and disclosure reminders

## Lightweight scope

This is a reminder, not an AI-use tracking or disclosure-audit system. Do not create an AI Use Record, collect tool/date/activity histories, add AI-use tracking fields to the Manuscript Contract or Decision Ledger, or automatically generate a disclosure statement. The author remains responsible for knowing and disclosing actual use. Do not ask an AI-history questionnaire before writing or review.

At final submission/resubmission checks and a full-manuscript handoff, briefly remind the author to check the target journal's current AI policy and disclose as required. If the journal and relevant policy are known, tailor the reminder to its requirements or verified exemption. With no target or inaccessible policy, give a general reminder and state that journal-specific requirements remain unverified. If relying only on a supplied or historical copy, briefly say its currency was not verified; do not turn this caveat into an intake questionnaire. Do not repeat the reminder for every paragraph, reviewer, or review round. A spelling-only request does not need a standalone disclosure warning unless its actual context makes one necessary.

A request limited to AI policy or a disclosure reminder does not start study-design intake, EQUATOR retrieval, F1–F10, a data audit, or reviewer rounds. If the author separately asks for help drafting a disclosure, use only facts they provide and mark unknowns; this is not a default deliverable or a reason to start retrospective tracking.

## Check the applicable rule, not an invented AI percentage

Read supplied instructions first. For journal-bound manuscript assistance, check the relevant current official AI policy with the other journal instructions when access is permitted; prioritize this before an activity that may be restricted. Use generic policy queries, never private manuscript text, patient information, or unpublished results. Reuse the Guideline Manifest described in [journal-guidance.md](journal-guidance.md) for **policy sources only**, not a personal usage log: source role `AI-use policy`, URL, relevant section, date checked, read coverage, and currency limitation.

Apply the actual target journal's rule to the current article type and requested task. Distinguish basic spelling/grammar checks, translation, substantive drafting/rewriting, analysis, images, and author responses where the policy does; do not assume a basic-tool exemption covers all LLM assistance. There is no universal permitted AI percentage. Do not infer one from a detector score; refer to a quantitative limit only if the journal explicitly provides it and the measurement is understood.

If the current request would exceed a verified restriction, tell the author which activity conflicts with which rule and pause that activity, while continuing unaffected permitted help. Disclosure alone does not make prohibited use acceptable. If the user volunteers information indicating past use may conflict, warn them and suggest editorial clarification; do not launch a historical investigation, certify compliance, or automatically contact the journal. A policy concern is not itself a finding of misconduct.

Recheck the applicable policy when the target journal, article type, requested AI assistance, or policy changes. This means reassessing the current request, not tracking a history of every use. Unknown policy is neither permission nor a universal prohibition. Honor no-network instructions. After one failed official alternative, stop retrying; use supplied material provisionally and request the missing rule only if it is decisive for the current activity. Statistical guidance, image-only AI rules, search snippets, or a navigation link do not establish that a full textual-AI policy was read.

## Confidentiality and review roles

Author-side simulated review of the author's own manuscript is not the same as journal-appointed review of someone else's confidential work. Before using or distributing commissioned-review material, establish journal permission and confidentiality conditions. Do not send the manuscript or a reviewer summary to external AI/subagents merely because the reviewer asks or the output will be labeled simulated. A generic checklist without manuscript-specific information can remain useful.

Independent reviewers may receive the same clean policy sources and permission constraints. They must not receive previous reviewers' findings or editor judgments disguised as policy annotations. No reviewer-by-reviewer AI activity ledger is required.

## What the reminder should communicate

Keep it to one or two sentences in the user's language, with a source link or access limitation when relevant:

- Before submission, disclose AI assistance truthfully according to the target journal's current requirements and retain human responsibility for the submitted work.
- The author should check required placement and details. Do not imply all uses can be called “language polishing only,” or that all journals exempt or require disclosure of the same basic tools.
- If a known restriction affects the current request, state that specific warning rather than offering reassurance based on a disclosure sentence.

Do not append a ledger, disclosure draft, percentage estimate, AI compliance score, certificate, or author-sign-off workflow by default. Keep the existing F1–F10 and five scientific quality gates unchanged. A reminder delivered is not evidence that the author has disclosed use or that the journal's policy has been satisfied. Do not declare submission readiness in the face of a known unresolved policy conflict.

## Official references and dated illustration

- [ICMJE: AI use by authors](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/ai-use-by-authors.html): publication recommendations on disclosure and human responsibility, not a single worldwide law.
- [ICMJE: AI use by reviewers](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/ai-use-by-reviewers.html): journal permission, confidentiality, and disclosure.
- [JAMA instructions](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/pages/instructions-for-authors) and [updated AI guidance, published 2026-08-10](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2852772).
- [NEJM editorial policies](https://www.nejm.org/page/about-nejm/editorial-policies); an inaccessible page remains unverified.

Illustration checked 2026-11-14, not a permanent rule registry: JAMA's linked 2026 guidance distinguishes exempt basic grammar/spelling checks from disclosable translation/content assistance and restricts uses including Opinion drafting and author responses to reviewers/editors. Check the current applicable policy at task time; do not impose JAMA's rules on other journals.

## Watermarks and natural writing

If relevant to the user's question, explain briefly that some services have publicly deployed text watermarking, but coverage and detection capabilities vary. Google's [SynthID text announcement](https://deepmind.google/blog/watermarking-ai-generated-text-and-video-with-synthid/) is one example. Do not claim every output is watermarked, promise reliable detection, or treat a result as proof of authorship or misconduct. Disclosure depends on actual use and applicable policy, not whether use can be detected.

The AI-Like Residue Audit improves clarity, specificity, and evidence fidelity. It is not watermark removal, an AI-percentage estimator, or a substitute for truthful disclosure. Do not turn the reminder into advice on evading detection.
