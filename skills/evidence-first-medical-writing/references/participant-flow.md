# Participant flow in original clinical research

Use this when asked to draw or audit patient selection, inclusion/exclusion, enrollment or analysis flow, including “患者纳排流程图”, “病例筛选图” or “JAMA-style flowchart”. The aim is to let a reader reconstruct **who entered which analysis and why others did not**. In manuscript planning, suggest a diagram if the derivation is hard to follow; in review/F4, examine the supplied diagram or flag a required missing one. A review request alone does not authorize a replacement. Do not substitute this for a PRISMA literature search diagram, a study schedule, or an outcomes chart.

## Reconstruct the flow before drawing boxes

1. Start with [design confirmation](study-design-confirmation.md), existing decisions and the [current reporting guidance](reporting-guideline-retrieval.md). Use the actual target journal's instructions; an article in JAMA is an example, not a universal rule. If classification is uncertain, ask how people were identified or assigned and keep the layout provisional.
2. Extract the source population, setting/enrollment window, counting unit, sequence of decisions, exclusions **at the stage where they happened**, group definitions, follow-up window and the named primary analysis set. Start with the earliest **documented** population; distinguish “all records retrieved” from “unique people assessed”. Do not invent a pre-screening denominator. Distinguish a criterion in Methods from the *number actually excluded* for that reason.
3. Build a private aggregate ledger: for each box record stage/arm, count or unknown, unit, source and locator (e.g., screening log v2 row 8; Results ¶2; Table 1 heading), and whether the source has been checked. Use already supplied information; no patient-level records or identifiers needed. Do not upload data to an online diagram service. Ask only for facts that change a branch, a denominator, or whether exclusions are disjoint. Do not call a guess author-confirmed.
4. Classify each branch: removal from this stage, retained group, outcome category, missing measurement, or change of unit. Ask **what was observed and where**. Absence of a qualifying local record is not evidence that someone received no care or was lost to follow-up. If the defined outcome is the presence or absence of that record, both categories stay in the outcome denominator. An outcome that cannot be ascertained is different again; follow the actual analysis plan.
5. Reconcile against Results, Methods/analysis plan, abstract, Table 1, primary outcome table, and relevant supplements. Table 1 can legitimately describe a **different** set: label and explain it rather than forcing agreement. Add children only for a confirmed exhaustive, mutually exclusive partition with the same parent, stage and unit. Adjacent analysis denominators may overlap; side-by-side placement does not make them additive. Overlapping reasons need a unique excluded-person total or a qualification. A missing count is not zero. Do not invent a reason or fill a discrepancy by subtraction.

| Confirmed design | Build this path | Avoid this mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Parallel randomized trial | Assessed → not randomized (why) / randomized → separate allocated arms → actual receipt, follow-up and **primary analysis by arm**. Show run-in before randomization when applicable. | Stopping treatment, withdrawing consent, missing a visit, and exclusion from analysis are different events. Do not subtract them all or infer an ITT set from completers. For cluster trials track clusters and people separately; for crossover track sequences and periods without counting people twice. |
| Cohort | Begin with the source population, show eligibility at cohort entry, then follow retained participants to the outcome window and the named analytic denominator. If useful, split by **observed** exposure. | An unavailable outcome is not a baseline exclusion. State whether the analysis uses complete cases, imputation, or censoring; do not subtract every missed visit from the analysis population. |
| Case-control | Define the source population and index period. Trace how incident/prevalent cases and eligible controls were ascertained, which were sampled or matched, and how many contributed to analysis in each lane. | Eligible controls not sampled are not lost to follow-up. Never infer a matching ratio from the final counts. If risk-set sampling permits a person to serve as a control and later become a case, label the overlap; do not force the lanes to sum to distinct people. |
| Cross-sectional or registry-based sampling | Show eligible records, linkage/deduplication if needed, and the response or measurement set used in the stated analysis. Use the appropriate cohort or case-control route for registries with those designs. | Do not interchange people, encounters and tests; neither weighting nor repeated measurements creates new participants. |
| Diagnostic accuracy or prediction | Show eligibility, index/reference testing or development/validation cohorts, and the participants contributing to each relevant estimate. Explain untested, indeterminate, and missing results. | Not every patient receives both tests; distinguish complete pairs from test-level totals. Do not turn internal resampling into recruitment or present resampled observations as independent patients. |

When an upstream denominator or an entire stage genuinely was not recorded, name that limit in the legend and begin at the first documented stage. Do not reclassify unexplained attrition as overlapping subsets to pass the checks. For protocols produce a planned diagram without observed counts; for reviews route to the appropriate literature-selection workflow.

## Decide what the reader needs to see

Apply a removal test to notes as well as boxes: if deleting a sentence leaves the reader able to follow screening, exclusions, retained groups, counting units and entry into analysis, omit it from the diagram. Do not move an unrelated baseline profile or a redundant operational definition into the caption after removing it from a box. Retain a brief qualifier if its removal would misidentify a branch or denominator, or if the journal requires it. Carry approved omissions into later redraws.

- **Title and caption.** Start with a brief descriptive title. If the title and nodes already identify the population, stage, counting unit and meaning of the branches, the title alone suffices as the legend. Add a short footnote only for a real ambiguity, such as a source-limited outcome, overlapping sets, a unit change or an essential time window. Do not add a long shared heading above a fork when its two labels tell the story. Keep full operational definitions in Methods, not in a repeated caption. Follow any different target-journal title, legend or reporting requirement; number by first mention, not automatically “Figure 1”.
- **Node text.** Lead with the **bold count**, then a short regular-weight label; use parallel grammar for parallel groups (e.g., “With a qualifying record” / “Without a qualifying record”). Use sentence case consistently: wrapping a phrase does not start a new sentence. Establish the counting unit once, then omit redundant “patients”, `n =`, percentages and parenthetical restatements unless they convey needed information. Keep qualifiers that change the interpretation, including a change from records or visits to people. A reader should be able to tell who remains in the named analysis without reading the ledger.

## Lay out the confirmed flow

- Retained participants continue **down**. For two peer groups, center the parent between them: one stem down to the midpoint of a horizontal bar, equal arms left and right, then a downward arrow into the top center of each child. The bar has no arrowhead. Parallel outcomes, exposures or analysis sets are not exclusions, even when a group lacks a visit or record. If retention is unclear, ask which denominator the group contributes to.
- **Only exclusions point right.** Draw a documented removal at its source stage, with a rightward arrow into the left edge of its box. This includes removals during screening or deduplication if documented as such. Keep connectors solid and clear of box text. Inspect the actual arrowheads in every editable output, including SVG and PowerPoint redraws.
- Use unfilled boxes, thin neutral outlines and modest padding. Use a common width down each column, matching widths for peers, and a common height for one-line boxes. Allow more height for multiline boxes or reasons rather than shrinking the type. Use the same readable font family and size for node labels and reasons. Center the **whole** count-and-label text block visually, horizontally and vertically. Within a multiline reason list, keep rows left-aligned together, indent reasons below the heading, and hang continuation lines under their own label rather than under the number. Centering the block does not mean centering every line of a list.
- These are editable design defaults, not universal JAMA rules. Use original geometry and wording, not a journal logo, copied figure or implied endorsement. Check the target's figure formats, dimensions and [AI-use policy](ai-use-and-disclosure.md) before proposing a submission file. Do not synthesize a diagram with an image generator.

## Render, audit and hand off

Work from a **new** JSON ledger and versioned output paths. Choose a synthetic example by design: [two-arm trial](../assets/flow-community-bp.json), [complete-case trial](../assets/flow-rct.json), [observational cohort](../assets/flow-observational-cohort.json), [cohort with overlapping analyses](../assets/flow-cohort.json), or [case-control study](../assets/flow-case-control.json). Never carry example counts, source locators or confirmation flags into patient work. Keep `synthetic: true` for demonstrations. Use `n: null` for missing numbers and `language: "zh"` for Chinese diagram labels and warnings (default: English); supply node labels in the same language.

```sh
python3 -B scripts/participant_flow.py study-flow.json --svg flow-v1.svg --audit flow-v1-audit.json
```

Run from the installed skill folder. The [local drawing tool](../scripts/participant_flow.py) uses only Python's standard library. Use `title` for the public figure title; add the optional top-level `caption` string only for necessary reader-facing text. Keep source locators, confirmation records, `notes`, and detailed relationship rationales in the private ledger/audit. Never copy them wholesale into a caption. In a submission-ready figure, explain a material limit in safe public language; keep teaching and unconfirmed working diagrams visibly marked.

Each node has a unique ID, label, count, source, confirmation state, role (`kind`: `main`, `exclusion`, `analysis`) and layout cell (`row`, `col`); the root unit can be overridden on a node.

Use `main` for a retained intermediate stage with a documented next step. Use `analysis` for each retained terminal category, even if its members are part of the same analysis rather than a separate analysis. For example, both “Sealant recorded” and “No sealant record” end in the same denominator in the [fictional dental cohort](../assets/flow-dental-records.json). Do not relabel an unexplained dead end to silence a warning.

Specify relationships, not just where arrows should point:

- `partition`: all children together are an exhaustive, mutually exclusive breakdown of the parent; arithmetic is checked. The relation does not set direction: two retained strata are both `main` or `analysis` and both point down. Place retained children in later rows, parallel siblings on the same later row in distinct columns, and `exclusion` nodes in a column to the right of their parent. Fix conflicting layout cells, not roles or counts.
- `subsets`: the children are not claimed to form a partition; each must fit its parent, but they need not sum. Explain relevant overlap or omissions to the reader. For a pooled analysis, retain arm-specific denominators and state the independently verified pooled total separately (e.g., in the caption if needed), rather than duplicating patients across incoming arrows.
- `unit_change`: the unit actually changes; supply the linkage or deduplication rationale. Do not subtract encounters from people.

To verify a within-box breakdown, give the node `reasons` (each with label, count, source and confirmation state) and `reason_relation: partition` or `subsets`. Use this for reasons for exclusion or for clearly labeled dispositions of those **already counted in that box** (e.g., observed vs missing outcomes among all analyzed). These subcounts are not later stages or automatic exclusions. Plain prose in labels/notes is not arithmetically checked. In paired testing, show both assessments as paths **within the same enrolled population** and the separate denominator with a usable reference standard; never count the same people twice.

The tool reports `ready`, `needs_confirmation` or `invalid`. It cannot read the cited source, prove eligibility or determine whether an author mislabeled an overlap. A checked diagram requires both the automated checks and reconciliation with the actual manuscript. If counts conflict or information needed for interpretation is unresolved, return a location-specific question, not a submission figure; use new filenames and `--draft` only for a visibly marked working drawing. If Python is unavailable, supply the same ledger, an explicitly provisional editable text diagram, and the checks performed by hand; do not claim a file was rendered.

Inspect the rendered figure at its intended width. Can a reader recover the population, units, exclusions and analysis denominators from the title, nodes and any necessary footnote? Check arrow direction and symmetry, lane order, text alignment, wrapping, clipping and agreement with the audit. Split a crowded figure into panels or move detail to its proper place; never omit a scientific stage to make it fit. Deliver the editable SVG and its title with any necessary caption, a short Results sentence naming the primary denominator, and the ledger/audit separately for the author (not public supplements by default). Report unresolved items separately.

## What the published examples teach

These are observations about flagship *JAMA* Original Investigations, not style mandates. No article text or artwork is bundled. Examined 2026-11-19:

- [Samieri et al., Three-City cohort, Figure 1 (2018)](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2697696): a vertical cohort path with stage-specific side exclusions ends at the primary dementia analysis; the two cognitive-change analyses have their own denominators rather than forming two mutually exclusive arms.
- [Moran et al., cellulitis randomized trial, Figure 1 (2017)](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2627970): the two columns preserve allocation through follow-up and analysis. Per-protocol and modified intention-to-treat populations are not additive; some exclusion reasons overlap.
- [Ng et al., PRIME paired diagnostic study, Figure 1 (2025)](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2838799): after exclusions, the same evaluable patients followed two MRI interpretation paths. Two parallel boxes do not necessarily represent different people or treatment arms.

For a JAMA submission, recheck the [current Instructions for Authors](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/pages/instructions-for-authors), especially the randomized-trial flow and figure-file sections. Other journals and designs may differ.
