# JAMA statistical-reporting profile

Profile: 0.7.1. Prepared and official sections read: 2026-11-15. Source: [JAMA Instructions for Authors](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/pages/instructions-for-authors), **Statistical Methods and Data Presentation**, including its subsections. This is JAMA, not an assertion about every JAMA Network journal. Future currency must be checked when a current-policy judgment is needed. Use with [statistical-policy-router.md](statistical-policy-router.md).

## Compact source summary

- Describe reproducible analyses, model variables and their scientific rationale, dependence handling, diagnostics, and software details. Pair comparisons with estimates and uncertainty, not P values alone.
- Give trial sample-size justification. Fixed-population observational studies need not have a power calculation; investigator-selected sampling requires justification.
- Report missingness and losses. For exclusions due to incomplete data, compare observed characteristics of complete/incomplete records in supplementary material. Consider imputation; the instructions reject last-observation-carried-forward.
- Separate prespecified analyses from post hoc work and address multiplicity or label secondary findings exploratory.
- Omit baseline-comparison P values for parallel-group randomized trials. This clause does not establish a universal observational-table prohibition.
- P-value display: below .001, use a threshold; .001 to below .01, three decimals; .01 through .99, two decimals; above .99, a threshold. Exponentially small values have a stated exception.
- JAMA's wording rule restricts causal claims to randomized trials, including a restriction for meta-analyses of trials. Treat this as target-policy scope, not a universal theorem about causal identification.
