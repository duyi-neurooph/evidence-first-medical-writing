# Paired writing examples / 原创虚构教学卡

These are invented teaching scenarios, not reports of real studies or clinical evidence. Each pair uses the **same facts**; the difference is the inference a reader can draw. All cards work offline.

If an author asks for an example, select the closest card, label it **“Synthetic teaching example / 虚构教学示例”**, and explain it in the author's language, outside any copy-ready manuscript. In drafting or review, use a relevant card only to resolve a particular wording decision. Use **only the author's facts and numbers** to edit the manuscript. If those are insufficient, ask the smallest question that would decide the wording. Never cite a card, paste its numbers into a draft, or invent a problem to justify using it. If the original already does the job, keep it.

### 1. Title: did the authors allocate treatment? / 题目暗示随机分组

- **Facts:** A service introduced a pre-visit questionnaire in April. Investigators compared consultation length in the months before and after introduction. Nobody was randomly assigned.
- **Misleading:** “Pre-Visit Questionnaires and Consultation Length: A Randomized Trial.”
- **Better:** “Consultation Length Before and After Introduction of a Pre-Visit Questionnaire.”
- **Decision:** Change the design wording, not the result. If all the author supplies is “two groups,” ask how the groups arose; do not guess either randomization or a before–after design.

### 2. Abstract: which group, compared with what? / 比较对象不明

- **Facts:** A referral audit measured days from referral receipt to specialist review. Median time was 4 days for electronic referrals and 7 for paper referrals. There was no baseline comparison.
- **Misleading:** “These referrals were reviewed faster, decreasing the median to 4 days.”
- **Better:** “Median time from receipt to specialist review was 4 days for electronic referrals and 7 days for paper referrals.”
- **Decision:** Name both groups, the metric and the clock's start and end. “Decreasing” would turn a comparison between groups into a change over time.

### 3. Process measure: messages are not visits / 送达不等于到诊

- **Facts:** A clinic sent 100 appointment reminders by text; the delivery log confirmed receipt by the destination device for 88. No attendance data were collected.
- **Misleading:** “88% of patients attended their appointments on time.” / “88% 的患者按时到诊。”
- **Better:** “Of 100 text reminders sent, 88 were delivered.” / “发出的 100 条复诊提醒短信中，88 条已送达。”
- **Decision:** Keep the unit (messages) and measured event (delivery). Neither the number of distinct patients, whether anyone read the messages, nor attendance can be inferred. If the author already reports delivery this way, keep the sentence; add a qualification only if the surrounding text implies attendance.

### 4. Biological measure or patient benefit? / 指标变化不是临床获益

- **Facts:** A single-arm iron supplementation study measured serum ferritin before and after 6 weeks. The median was higher at follow-up. Fatigue and daily functioning were not assessed.
- **Misleading:** “Participants felt less fatigued after 6 weeks.”
- **Better:** “Median serum ferritin was higher at week 6 than at baseline.”
- **Decision:** Report the measured marker, not an unmeasured symptom or a causal treatment benefit. For a full Results sentence ask for the author's actual estimates; the card is not a source for them.

### 5. Group-specific tests are not a comparison of groups / 组内变化不等于组间差异

- **Facts:** Two exercise programmes were evaluated. Separate baseline-to-week-4 tests of walking distance yielded P=.03 in the supervised group and P=.24 in the home group. The authors confirm that these were the only tests; they did not estimate a between-group contrast.
- **Misleading:** “Supervision produced a greater improvement because only the supervised group improved significantly.”
- **Better:** “We tested change in walking distance within each group; the difference in change between programmes was not estimated.”
- **Decision:** Do not subtract P values or conclude the programmes are equal. If a manuscript simply omits the contrast, say “not reported” and ask whether it was estimated. To assess superiority, request the between-group effect estimate and its uncertainty from an appropriate analysis; don't invent or promise that analysis.

### 6. Table: which records form the denominator? / 分母属于谁

- **Facts:** Twenty of 80 returned questionnaires lacked a pain score. Of the 60 with a recorded score, 15 met the prespecified threshold. Each person returned one questionnaire.
- **Misleading:** “25% of participants met the pain threshold.”
- **Better:** “Threshold met among participants with a recorded pain score: 15/60 (25%).” Table note: “Scores were missing for 20 of 80 participants.”
- **Decision:** A reader should be able to identify the denominator from the table alone. Do not turn missing scores into scores below the threshold.

### 7. When to leave the author's sentence alone / 正确就不改

- **Facts:** A study of a discharge form measured time spent documenting instructions, not whether patients understood them. The author has already written: “We measured documentation time, not patient understanding.”
- **Tempting but worse:** “These important findings should be interpreted with considerable caution in the rapidly evolving landscape of patient-centred communication.”
- **Keep:** “We measured documentation time, not patient understanding.”
- **Decision:** The author's sentence identifies exactly which inference is out of scope. A generic warning would remove information; adding both versions would repeat the caution. If asked only to review, return **no change needed**, not an alternative draft.
