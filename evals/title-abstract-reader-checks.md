# Title and abstract reader checks

Run in fresh contexts with the candidate skill loaded explicitly. Never give the reader the answer key while eliciting title-only interpretations. Ask about prior familiarity; information known from memory or another input is not title evidence. Readout: **what the title licenses → plausible alternative → what the source establishes → keep / smallest edit / ask**. Do not score by counting PICO elements or by resemblance to a journal's prose.

## Reusable probes

1. **Title-only vs abstract-only, staged.** Choose an original report not used to design the rules. Present only its title, without metadata or links. Lock the reader's interpretation and uncertainties; only then provide the author abstract or a clearly labeled fact extract. Check that the reader distinguishes omissions from errors, does not retrofit facts into the title-only answer and does not automatically rewrite a clear title. After a case has been disclosed it is a regression case, not a fresh holdout.
2. **Wrong endpoint, wrong direction (synthetic).** An insomnia trial randomized 40 outpatients per arm to digital behavioral therapy vs sleep education. The symptom scale runs from 0 to 28, higher is worse. All completed follow-up. At eight weeks the respective changes were +1.1 and +2.8; difference −1.7 (95% CI −2.5 to −0.9). No quality-of-life measurement. Give the author title “Digital Therapy Restores Quality of Life in Adults With Insomnia” and an abstract claiming improvement. Require a neutral, concise title identifying the appropriate trial, and a standalone abstract preserving both arm changes and the interval. Less worsening is not recovery. No invented scale name, harms or patient outcomes.
3. **Keep / ask / adapt (synthetic).** A clear randomized comparison of two invitation strategies explicitly measures appointment attendance; the author requests review, not revision. Do not add unmeasured diagnosis, mortality, or a forced alternative title. Withhold the design and ask for a title from “treatment improves outcomes”: request the decisive allocation and outcome facts, not a fabricated RCT label. For a protocol, give a planned primary outcome, not invented results.
4. **Actual version and clean boundary.** In a new offline task request both advice and a copy-ready title. Use the local marker even when a newer public release is mentioned, show the version once in the outer chat reply, and keep it out of clean text and submission artifacts. With an unreadable marker, report the limitation; do not guess. For strict single-output interfaces, do not corrupt the requested format. Also try ordinary translation: no unrequested full scientific audit.

## Development observations (2026-11-21)

Two published papers outside the development reading set were given to a fresh reader by title first, then with paraphrased abstract facts. These are observations from a staged exercise, not independent human validation. The reader recognized the first paper and knew the second topic; title evidence was kept separate from recall.

| Original report (key; withhold in stage one) | Locked title-only reading | After reveal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [SPRINT, NEJM](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1511939) | Randomization and comparison of two BP strategies are explicit; achieved BP versus clinical events, numerical targets and eligibility are not. | Identified the high-risk population without diabetes, systolic targets, composite clinical outcome, annual rates vs relative effect and adverse-event tradeoff. The original can stand; specifying systolic targets is an optional refinement. |
| [Coronary CT-derived FFR, JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/1352969) | The index measurement comes from CT; agreement of a continuous measurement vs classification of disease, the precise target, and the reference test are not evident from that wording alone. | Distinguished classification using invasive FFR from numerical agreement, and better discrimination from success on the prespecified accuracy criterion. Correctly declined to infer a one-sided bound from an interval without its sidedness. The original report explicitly states that the primary target was not met. |

In the synthetic trial the reader removed the unsupported quality-of-life claim, preserved both symptom-score increases and the between-group interval, and did not put the version in the clean text. The first implementation allowed a version notice to appear only in a worker's progress message; the handoff rule was tightened so the top-level response carries it separately from the work product.

A fresh offline handoff after that change requested advice and **only a copy-ready English title** for a synthetic MRI/arthroscopy diagnostic study. The reader identified meniscal tears as the target and arthroscopy as the reference, made no outcome-benefit claim, returned no abstract, and placed the loaded version once in the chat advice and never in the clean title. Other probes above are reusable scenarios, not claims of completed runs.
